Life as Project Management

Running personal and family life the way one runs a project: vision, budget, priorities, and milestones.

Published Wed, Nov 11, 2026 by Junqi Yang

Estimated reading time: 3 min

It is 2026, and I am preparing to manage my personal life and my family the way one manages a project. Why project management? Perhaps because I am getting older, and increasingly feel that life is finite — that one’s energy declines with age. Many things, if not done now, become very hard to adjust later when the body grows frail. Recently, spending time with my parents and looking back at photos from a few years ago, I have felt ever more strongly the natural aging of the biological body and the passing of youth. Now at the age when one is supposed to stand on one’s own, the sense of time pressure grows stronger, and many of the major decisions in life need to be put on the agenda. The older generation often says that there are things one should do at certain ages. Although the things they name may not be exactly the right ones, what is governed by biological law cannot be moved by personal will — for example, having children, sleep, exercise, and lived experience.

How do we use project management thinking to make decisions in life? Start from what you want to do — that is the vision. Make the realization of that vision into a project, and set a completion cycle. For example, what plans do I want to make for the coming year? For each plan, consider the resources required (time, energy, money) — call this the “budget”. Setting a project requires not only a vision, but also a look at the “budget”. Priorities are set along three dimensions: urgency, necessity, and feasibility. For my own coming year, there are three strong visions: having children, building the career I want, and spending time with my parents. Under each vision, the project content is set according to the “budget”. With vision and “budget” in place, the plan for the coming year takes shape. What preparations does the plan for children require in advance, what should one watch out for, and what are the milestone tasks? What do I want to accomplish in my career, to what degree, and at what point do I go all in? How much time do I plan to spend with my parents, and in what form — going back to my hometown to stay, or traveling together? Once the project content is set, one can list the action items and time nodes — what should be done by when for each project. The time nodes serve both to plan the project and to allocate time, attention, and energy.

For personal or family life, the point of project management is not to gain more, but to avoid missing more. Life is finite. A life of random walk may still proceed in an orderly way, but our wishes, longings, and pursuits, if missed at their better moments, may be very hard to reclaim. I hope I can live a new year without regret.
